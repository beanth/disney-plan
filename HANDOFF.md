## The 8/19 overlay reversal — read this before touching any Halloween date

**Guardians of the Galaxy – Monsters After Dark is running on 8/19, the only
DCA day.** The guide said the opposite from 4 August until 15 August, and the
15 August validation pass "fixed" it in the wrong direction.

What Disney's own attraction page shows, read off the live site by the user:

- Prose blurb: *"August 21 through October 31, 2026."*
- Date picker on the same page: **no times before 8/18**; **8/18 → 2:00 PM–6:00
  PM** (truncated by that night's Bash); **8/19 → 2:00 PM–10:00 PM** (DCA's
  normal close).

**8/19 is not a Bash night**, which kills the obvious "it's party-gated until
the season opens" reading. What it looks like instead: **DCA's Halloween
operation starts 8/18** — same date as Oogie Boogie Bash and Mickey's Trick and
Treat, one day after the Cars Land overlays — while **8/21 is the resort-wide
season badge and Disneyland Park's own start date.**

So the corrected model, which replaces the old "8/21 splits the trip" one:

| | DCA | Disneyland Park |
|---|---|---|
| Halloween operation starts | **8/18** (Bash 8/18, Trick and Treat 8/18, Cars Land overlays 8/17) | **8/21** |
| Your day there | 8/19 — **in season** | 8/20 pre-season, 8/21 day one |
| Décor | very likely dressed (inference — see below) | no entrance pumpkin, no Main Street décor until 8/21 |

**Décor at DCA on 8/19 is an inference, not a read fact.** No source gives a
date for decorations going up. The reasoning is that a park hosting a
hard-ticket Halloween party on 8/18 and 8/20 is dressed on the 19th between
them. The guide states it as an inference and should keep doing so.

**Unexplained and left unexplained:** why an overlay called *After Dark* lists
a 2:00 PM start. Don't invent a reason; if it matters, it's another question
for the user's browser.

## Decisions worth not relitigating

- **BB-series over R-series, chosen 4 August, and the clear dome was the price
  of it.** The R-series pick existed only to get a transparent dome (the R3
  look); transparent is documented in the *R-series* rotation and the BB colours
  that get listed are red, blue, purple, orange, white and black, with no clear
  among them. That's reported, not confirmed — so it's worth one question at the
  counter, but the guide should not send anyone hunting a part that may not
  exist. Also given up: bolt-on accessory panels are R-series only. **Don't
  quietly revert this to R-series to make the clear-dome advice work again** —
  the series was the decision and the dome was the cost.
- **Savi's over Dok-Ondar's.** Dok-Ondar's is a retail counter; legacy hilts run
  $159.99–400 *plus* a $44.99–49.99 blade, and there's no experience attached.
  Savi's includes hilt, blade, case and pin, and is the only path to a
  colour-swappable saber.
- **The double Blue Bayou stays**, deliberately. Thursday is food-forward on the
  dinner menu; Friday is drinks-forward on the lunch menu and gets the $34 Monte
  Cristo that Thursday structurally cannot serve (lunch ends 3:55). Don't repeat
  an order across the two.
- **Oga's runs last in Batuu, 11:15**, not first, so a slow queue eats the
  block's own slack instead of everything feeding the 2:30 Savi's deadline.
  Docking Bay 7 is quick service and only needs half an hour, which is where
  Oga's hour came from. If the queue is over 20 min on arrival, skip it — Friday
  night has it again.
- **Thursday's curb: all four claim it together at 7:00.** No splitting into
  pairs — that was tried and rejected. The parade doesn't reach the north end of
  Main Street until ~9:05 and Wondrous Journeys is 9:30, so 7:00 is two hours of
  margin against normal busy-night guidance of 90–120 minutes. The risk is the
  crowd, not the clock — final night for both shows, and Oogie Boogie Bash
  empties DCA into this park at 6pm — so if it looks brutal on the walk up, go
  earlier.
- **Friday's 1:20 Blue Bayou is load-bearing — don't move it later.** The 2:45
  Halloween cavalcade runs between "it's a small world" and Town Square via
  Matterhorn, the Hub and Main Street, so New Orleans Square is *not* on the
  route. The Hub is the nearest point at ~8 minutes. An 80-minute sitting from
  1:20 puts you out at 2:40, which just makes it. A 1:40 booking puts you out at
  3:00 and there is no later catch point. If the table ever moves later, cut the
  cavalcade in the same edit rather than leaving an instruction that can't be
  executed.
- **Carnation Café is on Main Street, and that's why it won.** You walk out of
  dinner into the Halloween Screams camping spot instead of crossing from
  Frontierland. Dinner service ends at **7:00** — you're out then whether you're
  finished or not, which is why the evening claims ground at 7:00.
- **Timings are deliberately two-tier.** Clock times mean something external
  enforces them (bookings, park opening, the 11:55 queue, the 12:30 Batuu exit,
  showtimes). Estimated steps show a **duration** in a muted style instead —
  "~100 min" for Cars Land — because a made-up number reads as an appointment
  you're failing. Each morning carries a *landmarks* line so being on track is
  still checkable. Don't "helpfully" convert durations back into times.
- **The Odyssey in 70mm is off, decided 15 August.** This will look re-openable,
  because the facts moved the *right* way just before it was dropped: the 70mm
  run reportedly extended to ~Sept 16, the seat crunch easing from ~Aug 20,
  matinees ~11:35/3:15 reported. It was dropped anyway. **Finding available
  seats is not new information** — the answer was no with seats on the table.
  Saturday is Downtown Disney and the drive to ONT.
- **Flag Retreat cut** from the Friday loop — 45 minutes of a 180-minute block,
  and a round trip inside a card tagged "no backtracking."

## Things that bit us, so they don't again

- **A "correction" can be the error.** The 15 August validation pass found the
  guide asserting Monsters After Dark ran on 8/19, checked Disney's Halloween
  post, saw 8/21, and corrected it. The post was right about the *season* and
  wrong about the *attraction*, and the pass never looked for a per-attraction
  schedule. **A season-level date does not settle an attraction-level question.**
- **The file was already carrying the right answer, in a comment.** A JS comment
  read "DCA decorates 8/18 and Disneyland Park 8/21, per Disney's own FAQ" while
  the card below it said both parks were pre-season. The comment was correct and
  had been left to rot when the card was "fixed." **When you change a claim,
  grep the whole file for it** — comments included — and treat an existing
  contradiction as evidence you might be the one who's wrong.
- **Marketing prose and the schedule on the same page can disagree.** Disney's
  attraction page says 8/21 in the blurb and lists 8/19 hours in the picker.
  Trust the picker.
- **Deletion passes drop facts silently, and the summary can claim otherwise.**
  The Monsters After Dark note was cut in the "delete whole cards" pass and
  survived only in a JS comment; the summary that turn stated it was still in
  the guide. **After deleting a card, grep for the facts it carried** rather
  than trusting a recollection of where they went.
- **Deletion passes cost hedges too.** Two "not confirmed for 2026" notes were
  once removed to make sentences read better, and a pinned-vs-provisional
  showtime distinction was lost with a deleted card. Don't trade a hedge for
  prose. The two 15 August cleanup passes preserved every hedge deliberately;
  where a claim carried its sourcing twice, one instance was kept, never zero.
- **A verification pass can cost hedges, and it feels justified at the time.**
  The 8/15 sweep removed six because fresh search results *looked* like
  resolution. The same pass couldn't open a single page. **An unhedged sentence
  should mean someone read a source, not that someone searched more recently.**
  Two of the six were wrong.
- **A snippet can be right about the thing and wrong about everything around
  it.** The "Caramel Apple Beignet" is real — at a *Downtown Disney* vending
  window, in *October*. **When a claim has the shape "*item* at *venue* during
  *dates*", all three have to come out of the same sentence you actually read.**
  The same splice produced the Blue Bayou pumpkin dessert.
- **Wrong-coast contamination is the easiest mistake here.** A Fantasmic!
  showtimes search returns a WDWNT article about *Walt Disney World*. Disney's
  blog serves parallel DLR and WDW versions of the same Halloween post and
  foodie guide. **Check the `/dlr/` path** before believing anything naming a
  show both resorts have.
- **Lightning Lane Multi Pass stacks.** The guide asserted the opposite for a
  long time. It doesn't replace; you hold several, and the 120-minute clock
  gives you a new pass whether or not you've ridden.
- **Three ordering bugs now, all the same shape.** The `DAYS` arrays drive the
  "up next" widget by linear scan, so an out-of-order entry silently hides
  everything after it. **Two invariants to re-check after any edit: `DAYS`
  events chronological per day, and `data-until` non-decreasing down each day
  section.**
- **"The backpack they come with" was never true.** The droid includes a
  cardboard box; the padded backpack is a separate ~$49.99 purchase. An
  accessory casually described as included is worth re-checking whenever it
  appears.

## Repo

`index.html` is the whole product — one file, no build, no dependencies.
`CLAUDE.md` carries the working rules. After August this is a keepsake, not a
live document.

**Commit and push straight to `main`. It is now the only branch, local and
remote.** A session on 15 August was configured onto a
`claude/page-content-cleanup-jedyst` branch, pushed one commit there, then
fast-forwarded `main` on request; everything since went straight to `main`. That
branch has been deleted.

Worth knowing if you ever need to tidy refs again: **this environment's git
credentials can create and update refs but not delete them.** `git push origin
--delete <branch>` returns a flat HTTP 403 (a refusal, not a flake — retrying
and backing off will not help), and the GitHub MCP server offers `create_branch`
with no delete counterpart. Deleting a remote branch needs the user, via the
repo's branches page or their own clone.

The page is more than static text. Day cards **auto-collapse once they're behind
you**: `prepFolds()` wraps everything below each card's header in a `.fold` div
at load, cards carry a `data-until` time and fold when it passes, and a whole day
section collapses to its date header plus a reopen button once the day is over —
defined as **3am the next morning**, because you're still travelling back when
the calendar flips. Anything reopened by hand stays open. Cards are ordered
chronologically so the next live thing is always the next one down.

**The fold behaviour is browser-tested.** `fold-test.mjs` drives real Chromium
via Playwright, freezing the clock at fifteen points from three weeks out to
after the trip, and asserts at each one that folded cards hide their body and
keep their header, that a closed day hides its cards and offers a reopen button,
that tapping a folded card reopens it and marks it `data-manual` so it stays
open, and that the day reopen button works. Run it with
`npm i playwright && node fold-test.mjs` (Chromium is preinstalled at
`/opt/pw-browsers/chromium-1194/chrome-linux/chrome`; the path is in the file).
All fifteen pass as of 15 August.

Editorial passes since have also been checked by rendering the page in that same
Chromium at a 390px viewport and asserting no console errors, no stray markup
from a broken edit, and the expected card counts (**32 total, 11 reference**).
That is a structural check, not a verification of any fact on the page. The rest
of the page's JS — the countdown, the sky, the accordion — is still unexercised.
