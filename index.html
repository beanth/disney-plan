🎟Multi Pass, dejargonified
  • The names first. Lightning Lane is the umbrella. Multi Pass is the per-day pass covering most attractions, which you've already bought. Single Pass is bought per ride, per day, and covers only two rides in the whole resort.
  • Rope drop = being at the turnstiles before opening and heading straight to one ride.
  • Return window = the 1-hour arrival slot the app gives you (e.g. "come 2:40–3:40").
  • You don't pick the time. Tap a ride, the app offers one window — the next available — and you accept it or back out and try another ride.
  • You hold one at a time. Booking a new one while the old is unredeemed replaces it. This is the rule people get wrong.
  • The next unlocks when you tap into your current ride, or 120 minutes after you booked, whichever comes first. Only take the 120-minute option if the new ride beats what you're holding.
  • The rhythm: book → do something else → ride it → rebook the second you tap in. Roughly one every two to three hours.
  • Book the busiest ride you can. Its window sits furthest out, which is where skipping the line saves an hour instead of ten minutes.
  • Rope drop what has no Lightning Lane: Peter Pan, Alice, Snow White, Mr. Toad, Luigi's, Mater's. Everything else you can buy into later.
  • Modify, don't cancel: refresh the modify screen to snag earlier times without losing your slot.
  • You must be scanned in to book. No 7am head start — that's Florida.
  • Single Pass rides, sold separately: Rise of the Resistance (DL) and Radiator Springs Racers (DCA).

🍪Quiet wins & early closes
Near-zero wait, nearly all air-conditioned. The things nobody queues for are what make an August afternoon survivable. None of these needs a pass, a reservation or a plan.

Walk straight in

  • Animation Academy (DCA) — a working Disney artist walks you through drawing a character in about 25 minutes; you keep the paper. Dark, seated, cold, and logged at a zero-minute wait. The character rotates through the day, so going twice is a real option. The best-value thing in either park and almost nobody plans it.
  • Turtle Talk with Crush and Sorcerer's Workshop (DCA) — same building as Animation Academy, both effectively no wait, good filler between class times.
  • Mickey's PhilharMagic (DCA) — a 12-minute 3D show, 5–10 minute wait because the theatre swallows the line in one gulp.
  • The Bakery Tour (DCA) — a cast member hands you hot sourdough at the door. Nine minutes, never a queue.
  • Walt Disney's Enchanted Tiki Room (DL) — fifteen minutes of aggressive air conditioning, a show every 15 minutes, ~7 minute wait, and the Dole Whip stand is at the entrance. Back row for the widest view.
  • Sleeping Beauty Castle Walkthrough (DL) — free, self-paced, ten minutes, almost never a line, and it ends on Maleficent and the dragon. Two flights of narrow one-way stairs.
  • Adventureland Treehouse (DL) — walk-on, and the best elevated view of Adventureland and Frontierland.
  • The Opera House (DL) alternates Mr. Lincoln with Walt Disney – A Magical Life; the Walt show and its animatronic are the ones worth timing for. Check the app.

  • Everyone building must be booked as a builder — a guest accompanying one may not build, so nobody converts on the day.
The clear dome is the R3 look, and it's a standard build part. Transparent is in the normal R-series dome rotation alongside red, blue, purple, orange, white and black — you are not asking for anything special. But the belt is luck of the draw, not a menu: put back anything you don't want, let it come round again, and ask a cast member to check the back before you settle for a solid dome.
If the belt won't produce one, buy it over the counter. Droid Depot sells individual domes and bodies separately — last published at $14.99 each, or $10 each buying three, so treat that as a floor for 2026. It isn't advertised or on display, you have to ask at the register. That's the guaranteed clear dome, and it's the fallback that makes the whole thing safe: build whatever the belt gives you, then swap the head.

Off property · the drive to ONT

You land late on the 18th and you're not here to spend money outside the bubble, so this list is deliberately short. It's really about Saturday the 22nd, and it points the same way your flight does — north-east, to Ontario International (ONT).
  • The Odyssey in true IMAX 70mm · Regal Edwards Ontario Palace, 4900 E 4th St, Ontario. One of only 25 venues in the US running 15-perf 70mm film, and it happens to sit in the same city as your airport — roughly 3–5 miles from the ONT terminals, call it ten minutes. From the hotel it's 35–40 miles and 45–60 minutes via the 57 to the 10, rental or rideshare — the guide doesn't assume a car. Those drive times are estimates off the addresses, not routed; check them the week before.
  • 70mm is the format that will still be there; digital IMAX probably won't. IMAX is reported to have committed the film to its general digital network only through 13 August, five days before you land — so the IMAX-with-Laser screens at AMC Ontario Mills and AMC Orange 30 have most likely turned over to something else. The 70mm run is reported to continue for months, with nothing else booked on those screens until December, but it's also reported it may come and go. Check showtimes the week before and don't count it as happening until you see the listing.
  • If you want it, book it now. 70mm showtimes were reported selling out roughly seven weeks ahead in LA, with resales listed above $200. Regal Irvine Spectrum is the nearest other 70mm house — about 15 miles from the hotel — but every park evening on this trip is already committed (World of Color, the final-night shows, the Screams debut), and it points the wrong way from ONT. Realistically it's Ontario Palace on the 22nd or nothing.
  • Budget the whole afternoon. The film runs 172 minutes, and 70mm prints were reported to carry a six-minute extended trailer on top of the usual pre-show — call it 3h15m–3h25m from showtime to walking out. With the drive to ONT and bag drop after it, that means a morning or early-afternoon show on the 22nd against an evening flight. Confirm the flight time before you book the seats.
  • Anaheim Packing House if you just want feeding on the way — a 1919 citrus warehouse with roughly 50 food and drink counters under one roof, about 8 minutes up Anaheim Blvd by rideshare. Cheap by resort standards, and it solves four-people-four-cravings in one building.
